    How it works

    Each check can be fooled on its own, so Lenzit runs independent ones and combines them with fixed, measured rules.

    Content Credentials

    Reads what the file says about itself

    ChatGPT and other tools sign their images with C2PA Content Credentials. A file that declares "AI-generated" is flagged, even when the pixels look real. Capture dates after delivery are checked too.

    AI-image detector

    Tests the pixels

    A detector built on CLIP ViT-L/14 and trained on 7,900 photos from six current image generators, with real damaged items as hard examples, so honest buyers pass.

    Product match

    Confirms it's your item

    DINOv2 similarity and feature alignment against your listing photo catch reused or wrong-item photos, and show where the photo differs.

    Decision. Declared AI generation means likely manipulated. A detector score above the level that only 5% of real-damage photos reach also means likely manipulated, and a moderate score means needs verification. A photo that doesn't match your item always needs verification.

    Measured results

    3,260 test cases from 200 products, scored only by models that never saw that product. The operating point is set so 95% of real-damage photos pass.

    What Lenzit doesn't catch yet

    • A screenshot of a small AI edit. The screenshot removes the credentials and the edit is too small for the detector. One sample shows this.
    • About 1 in 8 genuine buyers whose photo looks very different from your listing photo is asked to verify.
    • It doesn't yet check that the photo shows the damage the buyer describes.
    • It can't see the item itself, so a photo of a different but identical-looking unit passes.
